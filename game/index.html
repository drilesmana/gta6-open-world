CHAPTER ONE

Homecoming

The bus drops Dario at the promenade with a duffel and eleven dollars. Velora Bay still smells of coconut oil and hot asphalt. The garage on the west side is shuttered, and the sign says NIA'S in flaking paint.

His sister is not there. A note is: "Fare at the promenade. Don't be late. — N."

CHAPTER TWO

The Courier

The fare money was supposed to clear the debt to the marina crew. It doesn't. A gold coupe takes the case off the promenade at noon, and Nia's voice on the phone is flat: get it back before sunset or the garage is theirs.

CHAPTER THREE

Sunset Run

What's in the case is a deed. The marina, the containers, the whole west end. The crew wants it back and the department wants Dario. One car, three stars, and a garage door that has to close behind him.
